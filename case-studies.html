Case 01 · Retention

Reducing churn from 26% to 13% in six months

The business entered a difficult period with customer churn at 26%. The problem was not a single objection or one weak touchpoint; it required a consistent way to detect risk, prioritize intervention and help customers recover value before the renewal decision.

Baseline26%
→
Six months later13%

The diagnosis

Retention activity needed to move from reactive save attempts to a segmented, early-warning operating rhythm. The work had to show which accounts deserved attention, why they were at risk and what action was most likely to restore value.

The intervention

  • Segmented the account base by value, lifecycle stage and risk so outreach matched the customer context.
  • Used risk alerts and product-adoption signals to identify trouble earlier.
  • Introduced proactive outreach rather than waiting for cancellation intent.
  • Partnered with CSMs on tailored Success Plans for at-risk accounts.
  • Standardized talk tracks for save conversations while keeping room for customer-specific diagnosis.
  • Tracked churn, adoption, NRR and GRR trends in leadership reporting.

The outcome

Customer churn fell by 13 percentage points - a 50% relative reduction - within six months. The result came from a repeatable system that combined data, automation and direct customer conversations.

Case 02 · Growth

Generating $370K in expansion revenue

Expansion was treated as a continuation of customer value, not a disconnected sales event. By identifying where a customer’s needs, usage or business outcomes justified a broader solution, I progressed upsell, cross-sell and reactivation opportunities across the retention portfolio.

Commercial approach

  • Looked for growth and recovery signals inside ongoing success and retention conversations.
  • Connected the proposed upsell or reactivation to a specific customer outcome or adoption need.
  • Used account context and segment to calibrate the timing and depth of the conversation.
  • Kept expansion aligned with long-term retention rather than short-term revenue alone.
